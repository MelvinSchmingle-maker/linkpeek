Inside the council's budget plans

By Sam Jones

Millbrook council has approved two kilometres of protected cycle lanes on High Street, with work due to start in March after a narrow vote on Tuesday evening.

Councillors voted seven to five in favour of the scheme, which will separate cyclists from traffic with low kerbs between the old market cross and the railway station.

The plan was first proposed eighteen months ago after a survey found that most residents who cycle to work avoid High Street because of delivery vans and parked cars.

Opponents argued that the lanes would remove twenty-two parking spaces and make deliveries harder for the independent shops near the square.

The council says loading bays will be moved to side streets and that a review will take place six months after the lanes open.

Ruth Okafor, who runs the bakery on the corner of Mill Lane, said she was worried about early morning deliveries but would wait to see how the new loading bays work.

The county transport fund will pay for most of the work, with the council contributing the remainder from its road maintenance budget.

Construction is expected to take about ten weeks, and the council has promised to keep at least one lane of traffic open throughout.
