The river crossing gets a new footbridge

By Ana Ortiz

October 14, 2026

The council has approved a new footbridge across the river, replacing a ferry that stopped running after the flood defences were rebuilt in the spring.

Officials said the bridge would be open to walkers and cyclists by next autumn, and that the design had been changed to let small boats pass beneath its main span.

The footbridge is the first new crossing on the river in more than sixty years, and the council said the project had been planned since the old ferry landing was closed.

Engineers will test the steel deck in January, before the approach paths are laid. A public consultation on the paths runs until the end of November, and the plans are on the council's website.

Local businesses on both banks have welcomed the plans, though a shop owner near the ferry landing asked for a clearer sign to show the route while the works are under way.

The cost of the bridge, shared between the council and a regional fund, was set at a little under four million pounds, with a contingency for the piers if the river level rises again.

Residents who asked for the crossing said the detour to the nearest road bridge added twenty minutes to school runs, and welcomed the decision at Tuesday's meeting.
